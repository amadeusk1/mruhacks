# Current production direction

These notes record decisions made after the original design brief. Read them alongside the brief and concept gallery.

## Modular assets

Use one shared base bird and compatible rig. Hats, clothing, and accessories are separate, interchangeable equipment; do not fuse a bird and its outfit into a single model. Give recolorable bird surfaces their own material region, separate from cream markings, eyes, beak, and feet, so colors can be changed in-engine.

Architecture and props must also be reusable modules: separate building shells, roofs, doors, windows, signs, furniture, and activity equipment. Preview assemblies do not replace the individual assets.

## Visual quality

Match the squat continuous bird silhouette, broad facets, chunky proportions, and warm diorama style in the concepts. Check front, side, and three-quarter views against the relevant concept sheet. Independent art-direction review should assess actual renders, proportions, equipment fit, and functional clearance before calling an asset approved.

The initial procedural prop batch was rejected as inadequate and is not approved production art. The latest direction permits scripting and other efficient modeling methods to achieve the best quality quickly; the earlier hand-modeling-only restriction was withdrawn. Body color variants can be handled in-engine rather than duplicated as meshes.

## World layout

The world map is a reference for later layout work. It connects the central plaza and café/cinema/arcade with the sledding hill, snowball arena, fishing dock, basketball court, sketch club, and homes entrance. Its composition does not make every depicted dimension or gameplay detail a confirmed requirement.
